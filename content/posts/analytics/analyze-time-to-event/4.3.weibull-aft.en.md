While the
{{< series-link "posts/analytics/analyze-time-to-event/4.2.cox-ph.en.md" "Cox PH implementation" >}}
parameterizes covariate effects through hazard ratios, this section fits a
Weibull accelerated failure time (AFT) model to the same training cohort and
expresses associations via **time ratios**: the multiplicative scaling factor
applied to corresponding event-time quantiles under the fitted model.

For the underlying mathematical formulation, see
{{< series-link "posts/analytics/analyze-time-to-event/3.3.weibull-aft.en.md" "Section 3.3 — Weibull AFT" >}}.

### Reading the Coefficients

- **`coef`:** The estimated log-time-scale regression coefficient
  ($\hat{\beta}_j$). A positive value decelerates time-to-event (stretching
  survival lifetimes), whereas a negative value accelerates it (compressing
  lifetimes).
- **`TR / exp(coef)`:** The adjusted time ratio. Holding all other covariates
  constant, it scales every event-time quantile by $\exp(\beta_j)$.
- **`TR lower 95%` and `TR upper 95%`:** The 95% confidence bounds for the time
  ratio, derived by exponentiating the coefficient's confidence limits.
- **`se(coef)`, `z`, and `p`:** The standard error, Wald test statistic, and
  two-sided nominal $p$-value testing $H_0: \beta_j = 0$ (equivalently,
  $\mathrm{TR} = 1$).
- **`S`:** The Shannon information / S-value ($-\log_2(p)$), consistent with the
  Cox PH summary.

### Protective Factors

Contract length exhibits the strongest lifetime-extending associations. Relative
to month-to-month plans, the fitted time ratio is **4.832** for one-year
contracts and **15.493** for two-year contracts. Automatic credit-card payments
yield a time ratio of **2.121** relative to electronic checks, online security
yields **1.988**, and several additional service add-ons also correspond to
extended customer lifetimes.

The table below reports statistically significant protective factors (nominal
$p < 0.05$ and $\mathrm{TR} > 1$). "Longer event time %" is defined as:

$$(\mathrm{TR} - 1) \times 100\%$$

For example, a one-year contract extends corresponding event-time quantiles by
approximately **383.2%**.

<figure style="text-align: center; width: 100%; margin: 30px auto;">
<img src="/img/analytics/weibull-aft/protective-factors.png" alt="Weibull AFT longer-lifetime associations, with time ratios, confidence intervals, and percentage extensions of event times" loading="lazy" />
</figure>

### Harmful Factors

Fiber-optic internet, DSL, and paperless billing show statistically significant
lifetime-compressing associations (nominal $p < 0.05$ and $\mathrm{TR} < 1$),
with fitted time ratios of **0.199**, **0.300**, and **0.811**, respectively.
Holding all other encoded covariates fixed, the internet service indicators are
evaluated relative to no internet service, while paperless billing is evaluated
relative to paper billing.

"Shorter event time %" is defined as:

$$(1 - \mathrm{TR}) \times 100\%$$

Accordingly, paperless billing corresponds to approximately **18.9% shorter
event-time quantiles**—a direct scaling of survival duration rather than an
18.9% increase in churn probability.

## Evaluation

To ensure a direct comparison, this evaluation uses the identical training–test
split, training-derived censoring and Kaplan–Meier (KM) baselines, and 1–36
month evaluation window from the Cox PH analysis.

### Out-of-Sample Discrimination Capability

On the held-out test cohort, the Weibull AFT model achieves a Harrell's
$C$-index of **0.8545**, demonstrating strong risk ranking across customers.

### Out-of-Sample Event Probability Prediction Error

- **IPCW Integrated Brier Score (Months 1–36):** `0.09031`
- **Training-KM Benchmark IBS:** `0.15210`
- **Relative Brier Improvement:** `40.6%`

### Comparison with Cox PH

Evaluated on the exact same held-out test set, Weibull AFT achieves an IBS of
**0.09031** versus **0.09042** for Cox PH—a negligible difference of
**−0.00011** (Weibull minus Cox). Both models improve upon the naive KM
benchmark by **40.6%** (rounded) and deliver virtually identical ranking
discrimination ($C$-index of **0.8545** vs. **0.8554**).
