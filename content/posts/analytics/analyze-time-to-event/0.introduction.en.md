Most analytics questions ask **what happened** or **whether something will
happen**. Time-to-event analysis adds another question: **when?**

How long will a customer keep subscribing? When does churn risk rise? How does
that timing affect what we can spend on acquisition, or when a retention team
should act?

**Analyze Time-to-Event** explores the connection between those questions, the
mathematical tools used to investigate them, and the decisions they can support.
This page is the starting point; the series roadmap above links to every chapter
and case.

## How the series fits together

### 1 — Data Characteristics and Math Toolset

Start with the structure of time-to-event data, including censoring and latent
event times. Then build the mathematical vocabulary: survival functions, hazard,
cumulative hazard, and the modeling approaches behind Cox and AFT models.

### 2 — Business Cases

Move from estimating survival to framing business decisions. The chapter
introduces three connected perspectives:

- **2.1 — Lifetime Value and Acquisition Decisions:** connect expected survival
  to customer value, acquisition budgets, and payback constraints.
- **2.2 — Lifetime Phase Characterization:** examine risk windows across
  customer tenure and the lead time needed to prepare a response.
- **2.3 — Retention Resources Allocation:** bring near-term risk, value at
  stake, intervention effectiveness, and operational capacity into a
  prioritization framework.

## Choose a reading path

If the subject is new to you, begin with chapter 1 and continue into the
business cases. If you already know survival analysis, start with chapter 2 and
return to the mathematical tools when you need a reference.

The business cases are analytical framing exercises using the TelCo Customer
Churn dataset. They develop proposed decision frameworks, rather than report
validated commercial results. Predicting churn risk and estimating the effect of
an intervention are different tasks; useful decisions require attention to both
the model and its assumptions.
