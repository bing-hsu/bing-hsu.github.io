## Dataset Inspection

We use the well-known
[Telco Customer Churn](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/blastchar/telco-customer-churn)
dataset from Kaggle to get hands-on experience of developing the analysis.

**Raw Data Info**

<figure style="text-align: center; width: 100%; max-width: 800px; margin: 30px auto;">
<img src="/img/analytics/raw-data-info.png" alt="Telco Customer Churn raw dataset information" />
</figure>

The raw dataset contains **7,043 customer records and 21 columns**. Each row
combines a customer's observed subscription status with demographic, service,
contract, and billing information.

### Feature Groups

The "time-to-event" information is encoded in columns `tenure` and `Churn`.

We organize other columns into six groups:

- **Administration:** `customerID` identifies the customer and lets us trace
  records through the analysis. It is a data management artifact. We will
  discard it during modeling.

- **Demographics:** `gender`, `SeniorCitizen`, `Partner`, and `Dependents`
  describe the customer's demographic and household profile.

- **Contract and payment:** `Contract` distinguishes month-to-month, one-year,
  and two-year agreements. `PaperlessBilling` and `PaymentMethod` describe
  billing arrangements; `MonthlyCharges` and `TotalCharges` capture the monthly
  charge and total amount charged to the customer.

- **Phone / voice service:** `PhoneService` indicates whether the customer
  subscribes to phone service, and `MultipleLines` records whether they have
  multiple voice lines. The latter also has a `No phone service` category.

- **Internet service:** `InternetService` distinguishes DSL, fiber optic, and no
  internet service. `OnlineSecurity`, `OnlineBackup`, `DeviceProtection`,
  `TechSupport`, `StreamingTV`, and `StreamingMovies` describe the associated
  services. These fields include a `No internet service` category, which is a
  meaningful service state rather than a missing value.

In total, we have 18 candidate covariates (21 subtracting one `customerID` and
two "time-to-event" columns `tenure` and `Churn`)

### Exclude `MonthlyCharges` and `TotalCharges` in Modeling

By common sense reasoning, `MonthlyCharges` is the sum of prices of subscribed
services, whose subscription status is given by individual covariate, and
`TotalCharges` is simply `MonthlyCharges x tenure`.

This implies **strong multicollinearity of `MonthlyCharges` and `TotalCharges`
to other covariates**, therefore we should exclude them in following modeling
procedures.

Let's see if this assumption hold in actual data.

To my surprise, `TotalCharges = MonthlyCharges x tenure` does not hold strictly.
On the training partition, only **488 of 5,626 complete records** match this
formula.

Nevertheless, the Pearson correlation between `MonthlyCharges x tenure` and
`TotalCharges` is **0.999561**, indicating an **strong approximate
relationship**.

VIF (Variance Inflation Factor) analysis confirms my assumption about
`MonthlyCharges`:

<figure style="text-align: center; width: 100%; max-width: 800px; margin: 30px auto;">
<img src="/img/analytics/vif-analysis.png" alt="VIF analysis comparing predictors with and without MonthlyCharges" />
</figure>

Including `MonthlyCharges` introduces substantial linear overlap with the
service indicators. Its own VIF is **860.13**. <span class="orange">**Removing
`MonthlyCharges` alone reduces every remaining VIF below 5.**</span> The
comparison shows that much of the variation in monthly charges is already
represented by those indicators.

> **Interpreting VIF Thresholds**
>
> While exact cutoffs depend on domain norms and sample size, analysts typically
> rely on the following benchmarks:
>
> - **$\text{VIF} = 1$**: No linear relationship between the predictor and the
>   remaining independent variables.
> - **VIF between $1$ and $5$:** Moderate correlation that is generally
>   acceptable in applied modeling and rarely requires intervention.
> - **VIF of $5$ or higher:** Substantial multicollinearity that destabilizes
>   coefficient estimates and warrants closer inspection.

All above analysis confirms my conclusion of the exclusion of `MonthlyCharges`
and `TotalCharges` in modeling.
